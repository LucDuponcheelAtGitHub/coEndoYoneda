\begin{abstract}
We present a variation of the CoYoneda Lemma for functional categories equipped with a monadic
structure of its global elements, which we formally identify as \emph{Functional Categories}. 

The central theoretical contribution, the Pointfree CoEndoYoneda Equivalence, establishes an
isomorphism between, on the one hand, natural transformations mapping out of co-Yoneda endofunctors
to a designated kind of global endofunctors and, on the other hand, some corresponding kind of
global elements. 

Our original motivation was highly pragmatic: to formulate a CoEndoYoneda equivalence that could
natively support both pure programs (functions) and impure programs (functions with side-effects, 
modeled via Kleisli categories). While this aspiration ultimately met a mathematical wall, the
failure was profoundly revealing. 

The structural requirements of the \emph{Functional Category} equivalence categorically reject
arbitrary monadic effects. This limitation unexpectedly revealed a spectacular classification
theorem: the naturality constraint of our framework mathematically forces the monad to be pure
(idempotent). 

We present a grand finale establishing a perfect 3-way mathematical equivalence between abstract
category theory (in casu idempotent monads) and computational semantics.

In addition to outlining the mathematical foundations, we detail the complete formalization of this
result in the Lean 4 theorem prover. We discuss the technical challenges of working with functor
compositions under strict definitional equality and demonstrate strategies for navigating dependent
type theory to construct fully formal, pointfree proofs of higher-level category theory results.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}

The \emph{Co\!Yoneda Lemma} serves as a cornerstone of modern category theory, asserting that for
any category $\mathcal{C}$, object $X \in \mathcal{C}$, and functor
$F : \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Set}$, natural transformations $\text{Nat}(\mathcal{C}(X, -), F)$ are
isomorphic to functor evaluations $F(X)$. This result fundamentally states that an object $X$ is
completely determined by the morphisms out of it.

In computational settings, we frequently encounter categories equipped with additional structure.
Categories that have a functor mapping functions of types (or sets) to programs, their images being
called \emph{pure programs}. We define such a category as a \emph{Functional Category}. The world of
computer programs, consists of both pure programs (without side-effects) \emph{impure programs}
(with side-effects). In this paper, we explore how a variation of the CoYoneda isomorphism extends
in this setting. The variation replaces elements by global elements and is entirely pointfree. We
also specialize this setting to Kleisli functions (monad-valued functions), as such focussing on
\emph{pure computations} versus \emph{impure computations}, or, if you wish,
\emph{referential computation transparency} versus
\emph{non-referential computation transparency}
(cfr. \emph{referential evaluation transparency of expressions}).

Remarkably, we discover that the structural requirements of our Co\!Yoneda equivalence serve as an
airtight sieve for program purity, for programs that ase Kleisli functions. In other words the
structural requirements of our Co\!Yoneda equivalence serve as an airtight sieve for computation
purity (referential computation transparency).

We provide a comprehensive discussion of how these concepts and theorems are formalized in the
Lean 4 proof assistant using the \texttt{Mathlib} library.

\section{Functional Categories}

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a locally small category. We denote the category of types (or sets) as
$\mathbf{Type}$. For any object $X \in \mathcal{C}$, the covariant Hom-functor, which we will call
the CoYoneda functor, is defined as:
\[
CY\!F_X : \mathcal{C} \to \mathbf{Type}, \quad Y \mapsto \mathcal{C}(X, Y)
\]

\begin{definition}
A \textbf{Functional Category} is a category $\mathcal{C}$ equipped with the following data:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A functor $\Phi : \mathbf{Type} \to \mathcal{C}$.
    \item An endofunctor $GF : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}$ defined by \\
          $GF(X) = \Phi(CY\!F_{\Phi(1)}(X))$.
    \item A monadic structure on the globals of $\mathcal{C}$, provided by
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item a unit natural transformation  $\gamma\eta : \text{Id}_{\mathcal{C}} \to GF$
        \item a multiplication natural transformation $\gamma\mu : GF \circ GF \to GF$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

These structural components must satisfy the monad left unit law: 
\[
\gamma\eta_{GF(X)} \circ \gamma\mu_X = \text{id}_{GF(X)}
\]
\end{definition}

For now no other extra monad laws are needed in the formal Lean 4 proof. This is an interesting
observation on its own.

\vspace{12 pt}

This structure essentially allows $\mathcal{C}$ to reflect the category of types into itself, while
treating $GF$ as a monadic context. We define the \emph{Co\!Yoneda EndoFunctor}
$CY\!EF_X : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}$ for a fixed object $X$ as the composition:

\[
CY\!EF_X = \Phi \circ CY\!F_X
\]

We use the following definition of \emph{global element} as a morphisms from the canonical singleton
$\Phi(1)$ to $X$.

\[
G_{\mathit{\Phi}}(X) = \mathcal{C}(\Phi(1), X)
\]


\subsection{Formalization of Functional Categories in Lean 4}

In Lean 4, a functional category is modeled as a type class extending the standard
\texttt{LargeCategory} from \texttt{Mathlib}. 

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
abbrev CYF {C : Type (u + 1)} [LargeCategory.{u} C] (X : C) : C ⥤ Type u :=
  coyoneda.obj (op X)

abbrev CYEF_def 
  {C : Type (u + 1)} [LargeCategory.{u} C] (Φ : Type u ⥤ C) (X : C) : C ⥤ C :=
    CYF X ⋙ Φ

abbrev GEF_def {C : Type (u + 1)} [LargeCategory.{u} C] (Φ : Type u ⥤ C) : C ⥤ C :=
  CYEF_def Φ (Φ.obj PUnit)

class FunctionalCategory (C : Type (u + 1)) extends LargeCategory.{u} C where
  Φ : Type u ⥤ C

  γμ : (GEF_def Φ ⋙ GEF_def Φ) ⟶ GEF_def Φ
  γη : 𝟭 _ ⟶ GEF_def Φ

  γ_left_unit : 
    ∀ (X : C), γη.app ((GEF_def Φ).obj X) ≫ γμ.app X = 𝟙 ((GEF_def Φ).obj X)

  φ {X Y : Type u} (f : X → Y) : Φ.obj X ⟶ Φ.obj Y := Φ.map (TypeCat.ofHom f)

  φ_eq : 
    ∀ {X Y : Type u} (f : X → Y), φ f = Φ.map (TypeCat.ofHom f) := 
      by intros; rfl

  γη_φ :
    ∀ (X : Type u), γη.app (Φ.obj X) = φ (fun x => φ (fun _ => x)) :=
      by intros; rfl
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

and global elements are modeled as

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
abbrev G 
  {C : Type (u + 1)} [LargeCategory.{u} C] (Φ : Type u ⥤ C) (X : C) : Type u :=
    Φ.obj PUnit ⟶ X
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

A canonical, trivial instance of the type class is the world of pure programs without side-effects
(functions), $\mathbf{Type}$ itself, where $\Phi$ is the identity functor.

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
instance typesFunctionalCategory : FunctionalCategory (Type u) where
  Φ := 𝟭 (Type u)
  γη := {
    app := fun X => (𝟭 (Type u)).map (↾(fun x => ↾(fun _ => x)))
    naturality := fun X Y f => by ext; rfl
  }
  γμ := {
    app := fun X => (𝟭 (Type u)).map (↾(fun (f : PUnit ⟶ PUnit ⟶ X) => ↾(fun pu => f pu pu)))
    naturality := fun X Y f => by ext; rfl
  }
  γ_left_unit := fun _ => by rfl
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

\section{The CoEndoYoneda Equivalence}

The requirements of \texttt{FunctionalCategory} turn out to be sufficient to establish a
CoEndoYoneda equivalence for endofunctors.

\subsection{Auxiliary definitions and theorems}

We construct a designated global element from a designated natural transformation as follows 

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
def τx2ggfx {F : C ⥤ C} {X : C} (τx : CYEF X ⟶ F ⋙ GEF) :
  Global ((F ⋙ GEF).obj X) :=
    let ggfx := φ (fun _ => 𝟙 X) ≫ τx.app X
    ggfx
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

We construct a designated (natural, not shown) transformation from a designated global element as
follows 

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
def ggfx2τx
  {F : C ⥤ C} {X : C} (ggfx : Global (GEF.obj (F.obj X))) : CYEF X ⟶ (F ⋙ GEF) :=
    let τx : CYEF X ⟶ F ⋙ GEF := {
      app Y := φ (ggfx ≫ (F ⋙ GEF).map .) ≫ γμ.app (F.obj Y)
      naturality _ _ h := ...
    }
    τx
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

We formulate (and prove, not shown) a left inverse theorem as follows

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
theorem left_inverse {F : C ⥤ C} {X : C} (τx : CYEF X ⟶ (F ⋙ GEF)) :
    τx = ggfx2τx (τx2ggfx τx) := by ...
\end{lstlisting}

We formulate (and prove, not shown) a right inverse theorem as follows

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
theorem right_inverse {F : C ⥤ C} {X : C} (ggfx : Global ((F ⋙ GEF).obj X)) :
    ggfx = τx2ggfx (ggfx2τx ggfx) := by ...
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

Notice that both inverses apply $\varphi$ (defined as $\Phi.\text{map}$) to 
$\text{fun } \_ \Rightarrow \text{id}_X$, which mirrors the classic CoYoneda construction.

\subsection{CoEndoYoneda Equivalence}

Utilizing the auxiliary definitions and theorems above we can now define
the main equivalence.

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
def coEndoYonedaEquiv {F : C ⥤ C} {X : C} : 
  (CYEF X ⟶ (F ⋙ GEF)) ≃ Global ((F ⋙ GEF).obj X) where
  toFun     := τx2ggfx
  invFun    := ggfx2τx
  left_inv  := fun τx => (left_inverse  τx).symm
  right_inv := fun τx => (right_inverse τx).symm
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

This equivalence provides a completely pointfree and formally verified method for evaluating and
constructing natural transformations in functional categories.

\section{The Essence of Purity \\ (Every disadvantage has an advantage)}

\subsection{The Impure Aspiration}
The original motivation for this research was entirely pragmatic: we sought to define a
CoEndoYoneda equivalence that could play a role in the programming world, which encompasses both
pure programs (functions) and impure programs
(computations with side-effects, such as state mutation or I/O). A natural setting for such
programs is the Kleisli category of a Monad $M$, denoted \texttt{KleisliCat M}. 

We embarked on attempting to instantiate \texttt{FunctionalCategory} using this
\texttt{KleisliCat M} over $\mathbf{Type}$ to construct an equivalence that could natively
handle side-effects. However, this attempt revealed an apparent limitation: the equivalence cannot
be instantiated for arbitrary  monads. Our \texttt{FunctionalCategory} definition, while
structurally elegant, strictly rejected standard computational effects. 

But this disadvantage birthed a profound mathematical advantage: one of the
\texttt{FunctionalCategory} requirements acts as a classification of purity for
\texttt{KleisliCat M}. 

\vspace{12pt}

\emph{It declares referential computation transparency in a categorical way.}

\subsection{The Purity Sieve}
The core constraint responsible for this resistance is $\gamma\eta$-naturality. When evaluated in
\texttt{KleisliCat M}, this naturality requirement imposes that mapping \texttt{pure} over the
structure of a monadic value $mx$ is exactly the same as returning the value $mx$ \emph{without}
triggering any side-effects. Formally, it enforces the computational equality:
\[
mx \gg= \lambda x. \text{pure}(\text{pure}(x)) = \text{pure}(mx)
\]
We define this precisely in Lean as \texttt{EnforcesPurity}:

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
def EnforcesPurity : Prop :=
  ∀ {X : Type v} (mx : M X), (mx >>= fun x => pure (pure x)) = pure mx
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

One immediate consequence of \texttt{EnforcesPurity} is that the monad's shape functor evaluated at
the terminal object \texttt{PUnit} must be trivial (proof not shown):

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
theorem purity_implies_subsingleton_shape
  (h_pure : EnforcesPurity M) : Subsingleton (M PUnit) := by ...
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

Intuitively, \texttt{Subsingleton (M PUnit)} demands that the monad cannot express any meaningful
impureness at the unit level. For example, for the \textbf{State Monad} ($S \to (Y \times S)$),
evaluating at \texttt{PUnit} yields $S \to (\mathbf{1} \times S) \cong S \to S$. The only way
$S \to S$ can be a subsingleton is if $S$ itself is a subsingleton (i.e., there is no state).
Thus, State is successfully rejected.

However, \texttt{Subsingleton (M PUnit)} is a necessary but \emph{insufficient} condition for
complete purity. Consider the \textbf{Reader Monad} ($R \to Y$). At \texttt{PUnit}, this is 
$R \to \mathbf{1}$, which is \emph{always} a subsingleton regardless of what the environment $R$ is,
meaning the Reader Monad slips through this partial net. 

But our \texttt{EnforcesPurity} constraint is far more robust. When applied to the Reader Monad over
a non-trivial environment (like \texttt{Bool}), the equation 
\[
(m \gg= \lambda x. \text{pure}(\text{pure}(x))) = \text{pure}(m)
\]
fails, filtering out the Reader Monad as well. 

\subsection{A Universal 3-Way Equivalence}
If \texttt{EnforcesPurity} filters out every non-trivial computational effect, what does it mean
mathematically? By expanding the monadic \texttt{bind}, the definitional equation required for
purity simplifies seamlessly to:
\[
\text{fmap pure } m = \text{pure } m
\]
In categorical terms, this is precisely the requirement $T\eta = \eta T$, which is one of the
defining characteristics of an \textbf{Idempotent Monad}~\cite{nlab-idempotent-monad, borceux1994}. 

An idempotent monad represents a computational context where layering the effect twice adds no new
information over applying it once. In the category of Sets, it is a well-known theorem that all
idempotent monads correspond to full reflective subcategories~\cite{nlab-reflective-subcategory, borceux1994}, effectively forcing the monad to not
add anything.

We formalized this profound connection in Lean 4, establishing a complete, closed-loop 3-way
equivalence between \emph{pointfree} Category Theory morphisms
(the $\gamma\eta$-naturality of our Functional Category), Computational Semantics
(\texttt{EnforcesPurity}), and \emph{pointful} Category Theory monad-valued functions
(\texttt{IdempotentMonad}). 

\begin{center}
\adjustbox{max width=\textwidth}{%
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=huge, column sep=huge]
\text{Naturality of } \gamma\eta \arrow[rr, leftrightarrow, "\text{CoEndoYoneda / Functional Category}"] \arrow[dr, leftrightarrow, "\text{Abstract Category Theory}"'] & & \text{EnforcesPurity } M \arrow[dl, leftrightarrow, "\text{Computational Semantics}"] \\
& \text{Idempotent Monad } M & 
\end{tikzcd}
}
\end{center}

We completely verified this loop in Lean. The forward direction utilizes \texttt{bind\_pure\_comp}
to collapse the naturality square into purity, while the reverse direction beautifully relies on
\texttt{bind\_assoc} to inflate the purity constraint back into the full categorical square.

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
theorem enforces_purity_implies_naturality
  (h_pure : EnforcesPurity M)
  (γη_app : (Z : KleisliCat M) → (Z ⟶ (GEF_def (κΦ M)).obj Z))
  (h_γη_app : ∀ (Z : KleisliCat M), γη_app Z = 
    show Z → M (PUnit.{v+1} → M Z) from fun z => pure (fun _ : PUnit.{v+1} => pure z)) :
  ∀ {X Y : KleisliCat M} (f : X ⟶ Y),
    f ≫ γη_app Y = γη_app X ≫ (GEF_def (κΦ M)).map f := by ...
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

Thus, we confirm our ultimate thesis

\vspace{12pt}

\emph{Referential transparency is exactly the requirement for a computational context to admit a
global coyoneda endofunctor equivalence.}


\section{Proof Techniques and Challenges in Lean 4}

Formalizing abstract category theory in dependent type theory (DTT) introduces unique
difficulties, for example in \texttt{left\_inverse}, particularly concerning definitional
equality. 

\subsection{Strict Functor Composition}
In standard mathematics, functor composition is strictly associative. In Lean 4, however, these are
represented as distinct abstract syntax trees. They are \emph{isomorphic} via 
\texttt{Category.assoc}, but they are not \emph{definitionally equal}. 

When writing the proof of \texttt{left\_inverse}, this limitation prevents standard
term-rewriting tactics like \texttt{simp} from effortlessly reducing deeply nested compositions.

\subsection{Constructing Equality Chains}
To circumvent the strict type-checker without resorting to manual casts (such as \texttt{eqToHom}),
the proof for \texttt{left\_inverse} abandons the monolithic \texttt{simp} approach in favor of
explicit equality chains acting on the natural transformation components.

By utilizing \texttt{Eq.trans} and \texttt{congr\_arg}, we explicitly guide the type checker
through the unit laws and naturality squares:

\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\tiny]
  have h1 : 
    τx.app Y = τx.app Y ≫ 𝟙 ((GEF_def Φ).obj (F.obj Y)) := 
      (Category.comp_id (τx.app Y)).symm
  have h2 : 
    𝟙 ((GEF_def Φ).obj (F.obj Y)) = 
      γη.app ((GEF_def Φ).obj (F.obj Y)) ≫ γμ.app (F.obj Y) :=
        (γ_left_unit (F.obj Y)).symm
  have h_left_unit : 
    τx.app Y = 
      τx.app Y ≫ (γη.app ((GEF_def Φ).obj (F.obj Y)) ≫ γμ.app (F.obj Y)) :=
        Eq.trans h1 (congr_arg (τx.app Y ≫ .) h2)
\end{lstlisting}
\vspace{6pt}

This localized approach bypasses the rigid typing of the category morphisms by
acting directly on the equality type \texttt{=} in the universe of types. This technique is highly
robust and avoids the infamous "motive is not type correct" errors common in Lean category theory
formalization. Defeating type mismatches often requires subtle casting nudges, such as
\texttt{show ... from ...}, to force Lean to unify deep definitional equalities within higher-order
functions.

\section{Conclusion}

The Pointfree CoEndoYoneda Lemma is a variation of the classical Pointful CoYoneda Lemma in a
functional category setting. While our initial aim was to generalize this equivalence to arbitrary
computational effects via monads, the rigorous formalization process revealed that the required
categorical structure fundamentally filters out impurities.

By successfully formalizing the lemma in Lean 4, we have not only verified its absolute formal
correctness but also established that \texttt{FunctionalCategory} mathematically classifies
referential transparency, uncovering a 3-way mathematical equivalence spanning pointfree category
theory and pointful computational semantics. 

Moreover, we have demonstrated a concrete methodology for handling strict
composition equality in dependent type theory. The approach of combining explicit structural
definitions with targeted, step-by-step equality chaining provides a reliable method for
formalizing advanced category theory in modern proof assistants.
